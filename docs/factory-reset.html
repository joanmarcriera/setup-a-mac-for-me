Back up and erase a Mac

Use this before selling or handing on a Mac, or when you want to start again from nothing. Back up the whole machine, check the backup, then erase it.

Written for macOS 26 Tahoe on Apple silicon.

  1. 1

    Make a fresh Time Machine backup

    Connect the backup disk or mount the network share. If it is not set up yet, add it in System Settings > General > Time Machine. Then start a backup and wait for it to finish:

    tmutil needs Full Disk Access for your terminal app. If you have time, open the backup and spot-check the files you care about most.

  2. 2

    Export what you want to restore by hand

    Time Machine covers everything, but these are easier to restore one at a time than to dig out of a backup:

    • Keyboard Maestro macros and Karabiner-Elements rules
    • A copy of the KeePassXC database
    • Obsidian vaults, Logseq graphs and MailMate settings, wherever they live
    • Dotfiles and SSH keys
  3. 3

    Let the cloud tools finish syncing

    Check that Vivaldi Sync, VS Code Settings Sync and iCloud Drive have no uploads pending, and push any unpushed git work.

  4. 4

    Erase the Mac

    1. Open System Settings > General > Transfer or Reset.
    2. Choose Erase All Content and Settings and enter your administrator password.
    3. Follow Erase Assistant. The Mac restarts, activates, and opens Setup Assistant.

    Erase Assistant signs you out of Apple services, turns off Find My and Activation Lock, removes the apps you installed, and deletes every user account and its data.

If Erase All Content and Settings is unavailable

Use macOS Recovery instead. Shut the Mac down, press and hold the power button until you see the startup options, and choose Options. Erase the disk in Disk Utility, then reinstall macOS. Keep the Mac online and plugged in while it reinstalls.

After the wipe

  • For a clean rebuild, set the Mac up as new and follow the rebuild guide from step 2.
  • For a faster return, use Migration Assistant in Setup Assistant to bring accounts, apps and files back from the Time Machine backup.
  • Keep the exports from step 2 either way. Restoring one app at a time is often cleaner than moving everything back.

Commands come from data/install-groups.json, which also generates the Brewfiles.
